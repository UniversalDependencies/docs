The use of punctuation marks depends on the scribe. Sentence breaks are usually not indicated. There are no special symbols for marking a question, an exclamation, an intonation break, or focus.<br>
Some scribes highlight captions, others the switch from one language to another (typically in Avestan manuscripts with Middle Persian translations). Some scribes mark the content units, which may or may not concur with a syntactic unit.

- "And (it) is not possible to take one."
~~~ sdparse
ud ēk stadan nē šāyēd , \n and one take not is_possible ,
cc(šāyēd, ud)
obj(stadan, ēk)
xcomp(šāyēd, stadan)
advmod(šāyēd, nē)
punct(šāyēd, ,)
~~~

- "And when (she) takes a husband, (it) is valid as a marriage of high status."
~~~ sdparse
ka šōy kunēd , pādixšāy zanīh bawēd , \n when husband makes , high_status marriage becomes
mark(kunēd, ka)
obj(kunēd, šōy)
punct(kunēd, ,-4)
advcl(bawēd, kunēd)
xcomp(bawēd, zanīh)
amod(zanīh, pādixšāy)
punct(bawēd, ,-8)
~~~

- "For humans, the most blessed aspects (are) knowledge and donation."
~~~ sdparse
pad , mardōmān farroxīhātom tis dānišn ud dahišn \n in , humans most_blessed aspect knowledge and donation
case(mardōmān, pad)
punct(mardōmān, ,)
~~~
