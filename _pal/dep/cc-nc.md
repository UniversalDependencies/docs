The most frequent coordinating conjunction *ud* 'and' is written with a simple stroke that allows for other interpretations as well (e.g. a word-end marker). It is also often omitted in the manuscripts, and sometimes one finds additional strokes that can hardly be considered coordinators.<br>The connective *ud* can therefor not be established beyond doubt, but there are recurrent patterns, and comparison with New Iranic languages makes the existence of a non-coordinating *ud*, which only marks phrasal borders, likely.

It typically occurs after subclauses or hanging topics that precede the matrix clause and as a host of enclitic pronouns.

- "Why (is it that), when (they) do not allot the benefits of the material world on the basis of (one's) worthiness, *and* (they) judge the soul in the spiritual world on the basis of the worthiness of (ones') actions?"
~~~ sdparse
čē rāy ka nēkīh ī gētīg nē pad arzānīgīh baxšēnd ud ruwān pad mēnōyān pad kunišnudwarzānīgīh griftār kunēnd \n what for when goodness EZ wordly not by worthiness allot.3pl and soul in spirit_realm by worthiness_of_action tangible make.3pl
mark(baxšēnd, ka)
advcl(kunēnd, baxšēnd)
cc:nc(kunēnd, ud)
~~~

- "For whoever is overcome by anger can (once again) calm themselves through goodness."
~~~ sdparse
čē kē xešm aweštābēd u =š xwēštan wirāstan tuwān pad wehīh \n for who anger befalls and him self restore able by goodness
nsubj(aweštābēd, kē)
csubj(tuwān, aweštābēd)
cc:nc(tuwān, u)
~~~
