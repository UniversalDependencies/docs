`Degree` marks forms that morphologically express comparative, absolute superlative, diminutive, or augmentative meaning. `Degree=Pos` is not used: positive forms are unmarked.

Lexicalised derivatives are not marked for `Degree` when their meaning is no longer diminutive or augmentative.

### <a name="Cmp">`Cmp`</a>: comparative

`Cmp` is used with morphological comparatives of adjectives and adverbs.

Analytic comparisons with _ciù_ do not introduce `Degree=Cmp`: neither _ciù_ nor the positive adjective or adverb receives the feature.

#### Examples

* _bon_ “good”; _<b>megio</b>_ “better” (`ADJ`)
* _e seu condiçioin van delongo <b>megio</b>_ “his condition keeps getting better.” (`ADV`)

### <a name="Abs">`Abs`</a>: absolute superlative

The absolute superlative is normally formed with the suffix _-iscimo_. It occurs chiefly with adjectives and adverbs; forms of _tantiscimo_ can also function as determiners or pronouns.

#### Examples

* _o l’é <b>belliscimo</b>_ “it is very beautiful” (`ADJ`)
* _<b>beniscimo</b>!_ “very well!” (`ADV`)
* _<b>tantiscime</b> oe_ “very many hours” (`DET`)

### <a name="Dim">`Dim`</a>: diminutive

`Dim` marks diminutive nouns, including affective uses.

#### Examples

_Tambuetto_ receives `Degree=Dim` when it means “little drum”, but no `Degree` when it means “tambourine”. Analogously, _ommetto_ receives `Degree=Dim` when it means “little man”, but no `Degree` when it means “coat hanger”.

### <a name="Aug">`Aug`</a>: augmentative

`Aug` marks augmentative nouns.

#### Example

* _e scignoe, e scignoriñe e e <b>scignoroñe</b>_ “the ladies, the young ladies, and the big ladies” (`NOUN`)
