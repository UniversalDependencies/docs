### Definition
Pronouns are words that substitute for [nouns](noun) or noun phrases, whose meaning is recoverable from the linguistic or extralinguistic context. Pronouns under this definition function like nouns.

In Middle Persian, all personal pronouns have an oblique variant, viz. the enclitic pronouns. Only the 1st singular pronoun preserves case marking for the orthotone variant: direct case _an_ vs. oblique case _man_. Since the oblique form becomes generalised, it is not annotated as marked for case, only the exceptional direct case form _an_ is annotated as marked for case.

[Number]() marking is rare unless a form is inherently plural (e.g. _awēšān_ "those"). According to Early New Persian evidence (_ū_ vs. _vey_), the 3rd singular pronoun and demonstrative pronoun _ōy_ should have had a variant _away_ (or similar). However, it is not possible to distinguish them in writing.

Middle Persian makes extensive use of placeholders and resumptive pronouns. All pronouns can be used as determiners though some texts show preferences as to which pronoun to pick. Personal pronouns and the reflexive pronoun _xwēš_ are considered possessive determiners if they precede their headword. The relfexive pronouns mirror the person and number features of the subject.
- _<b>man</b> pid_ "my father"
- _<b>xwēš</b>_ pid "my, your, her, ... father"

Reciprocal pronouns are expressed analytically, e.g. _ēk ō did_ "to each other (lit. one to other)", _ēk abāg did_ "with one another (lit. one with other)".

The demonstrative pronouns _ēn_ "this" and _ān_ "that" form a pair in deixis; _ēd_ tends to refer to content, while _im_ is mostly restricted to fixed expressions such as _im rōz_ "today" (lit. "this day").

Relative pronouns, which are identical with interrogative pronouns except for _ī_, have developed to relative particles with the antecedent facultative resumed in the relative clause. Relative clauses with unspecific reference (so-called 'free relatives') display pronominal use, which suggests that it is actually the interrogative pronoun that is used for this clause type.

### Examples
- personal pronouns: 1sg. _man_ (archaic direct case: _an_), enclitic _=m_, 2sg. _tō_, enclitic _=t_, 3sg. _ōy_, enclitic _=š_, 1pl. _amāh_, enclitic _=mān_ (archaic _=n_ for pluralis majestatis), 2pl. _ašmāh_, enclitic _=tān_, 3pl. _awēšān_, enclitic _=šān_ (archaic _=š_)
- reflexive pronouns for all persons: _xwad_ (also as intensifier), _xwēš_ (mostly possessive), _xwēštan_
- demonstrative pronouns: _ān_ and _ōy_ "that", _ēn_ and _ēd_ and _im_ "this", _awēšān_ "those_
- interrogative pronouns: _kē_ "who", _kadām_ "which one", _čē_ "what", _kū_ "where", _čiyōn_ "how", _key_ "when"
- relative pronouns (mostly used as relativizers without pronominal value): generalized _ī_ and _kē_, and the interrogative pronouns
- indefinite pronouns: _and_ "so many", _čand_ "some", _was_ "many, much", _abārīg_ and _any_ and _did_ "other", _tis_ "something", _kas_ "someone"
- total pronouns: _harw_ "all, every", _hamāg_ and _(har)wisp_ "all"
- negative pronouns: _ēč_ "any"

### Border cases
Pronouns tend to appear in fixed expressions that can lexicalize as pronominal adverbs, cf. _<b>ēd</b> rāy_ "because of this" or "therefor", cf. the fused variant _zīrā_ in New Persian from _az ēd rāy_. _aziš_ as "from it" or "therefrom".
