This feature takes the following values: Fin, Inf, Part, Vnoun.

### <a name='Fin'>`Fin`: Finite
Finite verb forms are marked for person, number and mood. They head a clause or sentence.

### <a name='Inf'>`Inf`: Infinite
Infinite verb forms are not marked at all. They can be interpreted as active or passive. As a necessitative, they can head a clause or sentence, as dependent verbs they can head a subclause. They can convert to nouns.<br>There is a long and a short infinitive in Middle Persian. The long one continues the Old Persian infinitive in _-tanai_, the short one continues deverbal abstract nouns in _-ti-_. So far, it was not possible to identify a semantic difference. The short infinitive seems to be preferred in modal constructions.
- short infinitive: _raft_
- long infinitive: _raftan_

### <a name='Part'>`Part`: Participle
Participial forms are marked for aspect, which we call tense for traditional reasons. They are used in analytic verb forms (e.g. the perfect). On their own, they can head a converbial clause. They can convert to adjectives and nouns.
- so-called present participle: _rawān_
- so-called past participle: _raft_

### <a name='Vnoun'>`Vnoun`: Verbal noun
The verbal noun in _-išn_ can be used as a necessitative. It can be interpreted as active or passive. As a necessitative, they can head a clause or sentence. It can convert to a noun (_nomen actionis_).
- _rawišn_
