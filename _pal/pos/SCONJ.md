### Definition
A subordinating conjunction is a word that links a subordinate clause to its head. Middle Persian subordinators usually derive from relative or interrogative pronouns or a phrases including them (e.g. \*či-gauna- "what colour" developed to _čiyōn_ "as, like, how"). The interrogative pronoun _kū_ "where" can also be used as a relative pronoun and has become the subordinator of complement clauses. In the genre of religious commentators, we also find _hād_ (or _had_ ?, originally probably "being") and _ay_ (origin is uncertain).

Complex expressions like _ēd rāy čē_ / _this for because_, cf. German _deswegen, weil_ are decomposed and analysed separately as pronoun + postposition + subordinator. 

Relative clauses were originally introduced by relative pronouns but those turned to relative particles and are annotated as subordinators.

### Examples
- _kū_ "that"
- _ka_ or _agar_ "if"
- _čē_ "because, since"
- _kē_ and _ī_ (relativizer)

### Border cases
The junctor _čē_ is annotated as either the cojunctor "for" or the subjunctor "because".

A complicated case is _čiyōn_ "as, like, how". It does appear as a subordinator (marking the subordinate relation as modal) and as an adverb (answered by "so, in that way"). There are many instances, where _čiyōn_ is only followed by a nominal phrase. Since Middle Persian basically lost case marking for nouns, it is not possible to test whether the relation is one of a preposition or of a subordinator with the subclausal verb being omitted. To keep the annotation simple, such phrases have been analysed as prepositional phrases.
