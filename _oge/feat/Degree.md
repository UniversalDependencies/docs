Degree of comparison is used as an inflectional feature of [adjectives](_oge/pos/ADJ). There are three degree of comparison in Old Georgian, especially, Diminutive, Positive and Superlative.

### <a name="Dim">`Dim`</a>: diminutive

The diminutive degree (the so-called ‘-ish degree’) expresses a lesser degree, small size, or, metaphorically, affection towards the entity described by the noun. This degree is often used where modern languages would use the comparative degree.

#### Examples

* _<b>მო</b>თეთრ<b>ე</b>_ "whitish, rather white", _<b>მო</b>დიდ<b>ე</b>_ "biggish, rather big" etc.


### <a name="Pos">`Pos`</a>: positive

This is the base form of the adjective, expressing a quality without any comparison. 

#### Examples

* _თეთრი_ "white", _დიდი_ "big" etc.


### <a name="Sup">`Sup`</a>: superlative

The quality of one object indicates the highest degree of a quality and is compared to the same quality of all other objects within a set.

#### Examples

* _<b>ხუ</b>თეთრ<b>ჱს</b>ი/<b>ხუ</b>თეთრ<b>ეს</b>ი_ "the whitest", _<b>ხუ</b>დიდ<b>ჱს</b>ი/<b>ხუ</b>დიდ<b>ეს</b>ი_ "the biggest" etc.
