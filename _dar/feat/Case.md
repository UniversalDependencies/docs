Dargwa has three types of cases:
* functional: Ergative, Absolutive(Nominative), Genitive, Dative, Comitative, Causal (UD: causative), Substitutive, Replicative (UD: equative), Vocative;  
* orientation markers: IN (Case=Ine) ‘in a container’, INTER (Case=Inb) in a substance’ or ‘between multiple landmarks’, SUPER (Case=Sup) ‘on a surface’, AD (Case=Ade) ‘near’, APUD (Case=Apu) ‘in the functional area of a landmark’;  
* localization markers: ESS(ive) (Case=Ess) ‘no movement’, LAT(ive) (Case=Lat) ‘to the area denoted by the localization’, EL(ative) (Case=Ela) ‘away from the area denoted by the localization’, TRANS(lative) (Case=Tra) ‘through the area denoted by the localization’, DIR(ective)/allative (Case=All) ‘in the direction of the area denoted by the localization’.  

Locative markers are generally a combination of an orientation and a localization marker, so they are coded as e.g. _Case=Sup,Tra_ (movement through the the area localized on the surface of the landmark).  

Mehweb Dargwa has no spatial configuration markers for orientation in which the object is located on the surface of a landmark and stays there because of the nature of the contact between the object and the landmark, or because it is a part thereof (‘(a picture) on the wall’, ‘(a ring) on a finger’, ‘(wings) on the back’, ‘(a birthmark) on the face’). The markers for SUPER and INTER are used instead.   


## Functional cases  

### <a name="Erg">`Erg`</a>: ergative  

The ergative case marks the A of a transitive verb and the instrument.

#### Examples  

* _adaj-<b>ni</b> mašinka-li-<b>ni</b> muc’ur b-erč-ur._ : father-<b>ERG</b> hair.cutter-OBL-ERG beard(ABS) n-cut.hair:pfv-aor “The <b>father</b> cut his beard <b>with clippers</b>.”


### <a name="Abs">`Abs`</a>: absolutive  

The absolutive (nominative) case marks the S of an intransitive verb and the P of a transitive verb. It is also used in a binominative construction. 

#### Examples  

* _<b>ʡali</b> w-ak’-ib_ : Ali(Abs) M-come:PFV-AOR “The fish is <b>in the river</b>.”


### <a name="Gen">`Gen`</a>: genitive  

#### Examples  

* _rasuj-ni ar-d-uk-ib <b>muħammad-la</b> kwihme_ : Rasul.OBL-ERG away-NPL-lead:PFV-AOR Muhammad-<b>GEN</b> sheep.PL “Rasul took away <b>Muhammad’s</b> sheep.”


### <a name="Dat">`Dat`</a>: dative  

#### Examples  

* _abaj-ni gi-b sadaq’ači-li-<b>s</b> t’ult’_ : mother-ERG give:PFV-AOR pauper-OBL-<b>DAT</b> bread “Mother gave bread <b>to a pauper</b>.”


### <a name="Com">`Com`</a>: comitative  

Comitative is used for co-participants and instruments, including consumables.

#### Examples  

* _rasul urʁes w-ik-ib muħammadi-<b>ču</b>_ : Rasul fight:IPFV-INF M-LV:PFV-AOR Muhammad-<b>COMIT</b> “Rasul fought <b>with Muhammad</b>.”


### <a name="Cau">`Cau`</a>: causal (causative / motivative / purposive)  

Causal not attested in Modern Megweb. 
  
#### Examples  

* _? di-la χuligan-deši-<b>čible</b> nu tusnaq’ w-aq’-ib._ : I.OBL-GEN hooligan-NMLZ-<b>CAUSAL</b> I arrest M-do:PFV-AOR “I got arrested because <b>of my hooliganism</b>.” (Magometov 1982: 49)  


### <a name="Cns">`Cns`</a>: considerative / substitutive   

Substitutive is used when the actor performs an action instead of someone who was supposed to perform it.  

#### Examples  

* _nu adaj-<b>čemadal</b> tukaj-ħe w-aˤq'-un-na_ : I father-<b>SUBST</b> shop.OBL-IN(LAT) M-go:PFV-AOR-EGO “I went to the shop <b>instead of father</b>.”


### <a name="Equ">`Equ`</a>: replicative (equative)  

The equative case means “X-like”, “similar to X”, “same as X”. It conveys the semantics of performing an action in the way similar to how someone or something else performs it, or in the way it is usually done. The form attaches to an irregular oblique stem.

#### Examples

* _dilaj-<b>sum</b> b-aq’-a_ : I.OBL-REPL N-do:PFV-IMP.TR “Do <b>as I</b> do.”  


### <a name="Voc">`Voc`</a>: vocative  

A special vocative form only exists for two-syllable stems which denote humans. These forms are treated as a special stress pattern, with the stress (or special vocative intonation) on the first syllable.

#### Examples

* _adáj_ (Absolutive) “father” ~ _ádaj_ (Vocative) “fatherǃ”



## Orientation  

### <a name="Ine">`Ine`</a>: inessive   

IN ‘in a container’

### <a name="Sup">`Sup`</a>: superessive    

SUPER ‘on a surface’

### <a name="Ade">`Ade`</a>: adessive  

AD ‘near’


### <a name="Inb">`Inb`</a>: inter(essive)  

INTER denotes the configuration when an object is within the spatial area of the landmark and the landmark is either a substance or a set of objects (e.g. in the forest).  

In addition, these case forms can denote contact between the object and the landmark, a participant who becomes the agent or cause of a situation unintentionally (involuntary agent), temporary possessor, temporary recipient and the addressee with verbs of speech.  

#### Examples

* Case=Inb,Ess: _k’as ħark’wi-<b>ze</b>-b le-b_ : fish river-INTER-N(ESS) be-N “The fish is <b>in the river</b>.”
* Case=Inb, Lat: _muħammadi-<b>ze</b>_ : Muhammad-inter(lat) “(to say smth.) to Muhammad”  


### <a name="Apu">`Apu`</a>: Apud case    

The Apud case is used to denote an area close to an object, in which the figure must be located to interact with the object (functional proximity). It is only compatible with words designating landmarks that have an area associated with them in this way, e.g. desk/table, water source, house. Apud can also be used to designate the area not near to but bounded by the landmark (e.g. door in example below) and an exchange equivalent.  

#### Examples

* Case=Ess,Inb: _nuša ustuj-<b>ʡeʕ</b>-b ka-b-iʔ-i-ra_ : we table.OBL-<b>APUD</b>-HPL(ESS) PV-HPL-sit:PFV-AOR-1/2 “We are sitting <b>at the table</b>.”
* Case=Inb,Tra: _škaf unza-le-<b>ʡeʕ</b>-di b-aʕq’-un_ : wardrobe door-OBL-APUD-TRANS N-go:PFV-AOR “The wardrobe went <b>through the door</b>.”  



## Location and direction

### <a name="Ess">`Ess`</a>: esssive   

ESS ‘no movement’

### <a name="Lat">`Lat`</a>: lative  

LAT ‘to the area denoted by the localization

### <a name="Ela">`Ela`</a>: elative    

EL ‘away from the area denoted by the localization’

### <a name="Tra">`Tra`</a>: translative    

TRANS ‘through the area denoted by the localization’

### <a name="All">`All`</a>: directive (allative)    

DIR ‘in the direction of the area denoted by the localization’


## Notes

The Replicative case is equal to nominative (unmarked form) and is not tagged in the Mehweb treebank.  
Orientation cases `Inb` and `Apu` are language-specific.
