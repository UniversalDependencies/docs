A non-canonical nominal subject is a noun phrase in a case form that is not typical for subjects. The verbs with the non-canonical subject includes:  
* dative subject verbs with dative subject, e.g. _biges_ ‘love, want’, _bikes_ ‘happen’, _eba buhes_ ‘get bored’, _určeb leb_ ‘remember’, _urče bak’as_ ‘recall’, _urče bikes_ ‘recall’, _qumartes_ ‘forget’;  
* inter-lative subject verbs, e.g. _gʷes_ ‘see’, _arʁes_ ‘hear’, _bahes_ ‘know’, _barges_ ‘find’, _qumartes_ ‘forget’;  
* inter-elative subject verb _buhes_ ‘manage, be able’.  

~~~ sdparse
ʡalize deč' qumartur . \n Ali forgot the song .
nsubj:nc(qumartur, ʡalize)
nsubj:nc(forgot, Ali)
~~~

Ali-INTER(LAT) song(ABS) forget-LV:PF-AOR


~~~ sdparse
ʡalis deč' qumartur . \n Ali forgot the song .
nsubj:nc(qumartur, ʡalis)
nsubj:nc(forgot, Ali)
~~~

Ali-DAT song(ABS) forget-LV:PF-AOR  

In a biabsolutive construction where both core arguments of the transitive verb (subject and direct) object are expressed in the absolutive case, the subject is treated as a non-canoncical subject.  

~~~ sdparse

adami kʷiha berhuwe lew . \n The man slaughtered a ram .
nsubj:nc(berhuwe, adami)
nsubj:nc(slaughtered, man)
~~~

man ram N-slaughter:PFV-PRS-CVB AUX-M
