The feature `NounClass` refers to grammatical agreement marked in Chechen on a subset of verbs, adjectives, and numerals.
There are four primary prefixes that appear on the agreement targets: _v-_, _j-_, _d-_, and _b-_.
A pair of singular and plural agreement prefixes define a grammatical gender.
Komen et al. (2021: 324-325) describe a system of five Chechen genders:

| Gender | Singular | Plural | Notes |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| 1st/2nd person | V/J | D | V for male, J for female; pronouns only | 
| Human | V/J | B | V for male, J for female | 
| Various | B | B | Minority of B gender |
| Various | D | D | Minority of D gender |
| Various | J | J |  | 

Chechen has ergative-absolutive alignment. 
Predicates agree in noun class with the absolutive argument, commonly the subject of intransitive predicates or the object of transitive predicates.


### <a name="Vclass">`Vclass`</a>: V-class

The V-class marks agreement with constituents denoting singular human male referents. It is marked with the prefix _v-_.

#### Examples

* _<b>v</b>edda_ "(he) ran away"


### <a name="Jclass">`Jclass`</a>: J-class

The J-class marks agreement with constituents denoting singular human female referents, as well as animals and inanimate entities. It is marked with the prefix _j-_.

#### Examples

*_<b>j</b>edda_ "(she) ran away"


### <a name="Dclass">`Dclass`</a>: D-class

The D-class marks agreement with constituents denoting animals and inanimate entities. It is marked with the prefix _d-_. 
This class is used as the default citation form for all agreeing predicates.

#### Examples

* _sa <b>d</b>ajna_ "light vanished (it got dark)"


### <a name="Bclass">`Bclass`</a>: B-class

The B-class marks agreement with constituents denoting animals and inanimate objects, as well as plural human referents. 
It is marked with the prefix _b-_.

#### Examples

* _<b>b<\b>axch_ "when they went"
