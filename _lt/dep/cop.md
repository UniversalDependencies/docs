A `cop` (copula) is the relation between the nominal predicate and the copular verb _būti_ “to be”. We normally take a copula as a dependent of its complement (the nominal predicate).

~~~ sdparse
Jis yra mandagus . \n He is polite .
cop(mandagus, yra)
cop(polite, is)
~~~

~~~ sdparse
Jis yra mandagus vaikas . \n He is a-polite child .
cop(vaikas, yra)
cop(child, is)
~~~

The nominal predicate is usually a noun, an adjective (or an adjectival participle), more rarely – adverb, pronoun or numeral.

_Būti (‘to-be’) _+ noun:
~~~ sdparse
Ji yra mama . \n She is a-mother .
cop(mama, yra)
cop(mother, is)
~~~

~~~ sdparse
Ji yra namuose . \n She is at-home .
cop(namuose, yra)
cop(at-home, is)
~~~

_Būti (‘to-be’)_ + adjective: 
~~~ sdparse
Ji yra graži . \n She is beautiful .
cop(graži, yra)
cop(beautiful, is)
~~~

_Būti (‘to-be’) _+ participle: 
~~~ sdparse
Šis variantas yra galimas . \n This variant is possible .
cop(galimas, yra)
cop(possible, is)
~~~

_Būti (‘to-be’)_ + adverb:
~~~ sdparse
Jai yra gerai . \n She is well .
cop(gerai, yra)
cop(well, is)
~~~

_Būti (‘to-be’)_ + pronoun: 
~~~ sdparse
Ji buvo kitokia . \n She was different .
cop(kitokia, buvo)
cop(different, was)
~~~

_Būti (‘to-be’)_ + skaitvardis:
~~~ sdparse
Jos yra dviese . \n They are two .
cop(dviese, yra)
Cop(two, are)
~~~
