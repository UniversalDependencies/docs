### <a name="Fin">`Fin`</a>: finite verb

Rule of thumb: if it has non-empty [Mood](), it is finite. In Middle Armenian this applies to conditional (also to its [connegative](Connegative) form), indicative, imperative and subjunctive forms.

#### Examples

* _գիտեմ_/_gitem_ “I know”, _գիտես_/_gites_ “you know”, _գիտէ_/_gitē_ “he/she/it knows”, _գիտեմք (գիտենք)_/_gitemkʼ (gitenkʼ)_ “we know”, _գիտէք_/_gitēkʼ_ “you know”, _գիտեն_/_giten_ “they know”,
* _մնացի_/_mnacʼi_ “I stayed”, _մնացեր_/_mnacʼer_ “you stayed”, _մնաց_ “he/she/it stayed”, _մնացաք_/_mnacʼakʼ_ “we stayed”, _մնացէք_/_mnacʼēkʼ_ “you stayed”, _մնացին_/_mnacʼin_ “they stayed”,
* _եմ_/_em_ “I am”, _ես_/_es_ “you are”, _է_/_ē_ “he/she/it is”, _եմք_/_emkʼ_ “we are”, _էք_/_ēkʼ_ “you are”, _են_/_en_ “they are”,
* _աղա՛_/_aġa՛_, _աղացե՛ք_/_aġacʼe՛kʼ_ “grind!” (imperative in different numbers),
* _գրեմ_/_grem_ “I write `Sub`”, _գրես_/_gres_ “you write `Sub`”, _գրէ_/_grē_ “he/she/it write `Sub`”, _գրեմք_/_gremkʼ_ “we write `Sub`”, _գրէք_/_grēkʼ_ “you write `Sub`”, _գրեն_/_gren_ “they write `Sub`”

### <a name="Inf">`Inf`</a>: infinitive

The infinitive is the citation form of verbs. In Middle Armenian, it is a non-finite verb form. It can occur as an argument of other verbs. When used with the auxiliary _եմ_/_em_ and the preposition _ի_/_i_, it forms the present indicative.

The infinitive shares properties of verbs and nouns. It inflects for [Case](), [Definite](), [Number](), [Number[psor]]() and [Person[psor]]().
Note that in Middle Armenian grammar, the genitive-dative case of the infinitive is treated as a future participle, regardless of whether it functions as a verbal tense (with an auxiliary verb) or as a modifier (with a noun). However, we apply the principle of Modern Armenian (Eastern and Western), where only the form used with an auxiliary (to create the future tense of the indicative mood) is considered a future participle.

#### Examples

* _<b>ի գալ է</b>_/_i gal ē_ “it comes; it is coming”
* _Որով զոք դատիս՝ Նոյն դատաստան կամիս <b>կրել</b>_/_Orov zok’ datis՝ Noyn datastan kamis krel_ “The judgment you pass on others is the same one you are willing <b>to face</b> yourself”,
* _Պատրաստ եմ <b>մեռանել</b> վասն անուան նորին_/_Patrast em meṙanel vasn anowan norin_ “I am ready to die for his name”,
* _Մի՞թէ <b>մեռանելոյ</b> օրս մոտեցաւ_/_mit’ē meraneloy òrs motec’aw_ “Has my day <b>to die</b> really come”,
* _Եւ զայդ ի <b>դողալուն</b> ատենէ սկիզբն արա_/_ew zayd i doġalown atenē skizbn ara_ “And start this from the moment of <b>trembling</b>”

  
### <a name="Part">`Part`</a>: participle, verbal adjective

Participles are non-finite verb forms that share properties of verbs and adjectives. In Middle Armenian, some participles may be used to form complex tenses and can also be used adjectively. Nevertheless, they are always annotated as [verb](VERB).

Note that the so called _negative participle_ is used to form negated periphrastic indicative and conditional forms. We mark them [Connegative]().

Middle Armenian has six types of participles:

- The resultative participle is used to form the indicative mood in present and imperfect resultative. It can be used adjectively and can be negated.
- The subject participle can be used only adjectively and can be negated.
- The imperfective participle is used to form the indicative mood in present or imperfect tense. It is formed with two endings: _-իս/-is_, which is attached to the infinitive, and _-ում/-owm_, which is attached to the verb stem.
- The future participle is used to form the indicative mood in present future or imperfect future (future-in-the-past) tense. Some of its forms (mainly with the ending _-ի/-i_ attached to the infinitive) can be used only adjectively and can be negated.
- The past participle continues the Classical Armenian past participle. While in Classical Armenian this form could have both active and passive meanings, in Middle Armenian it increasingly tends toward a passive interpretation. A new passive infix (_-ու-_/_-ow-_) has also emerged. It can be used adjectivally or substantivally.
- The perfect participle is used to form the indicative mood in present perfect or imperfect-in-the-past (past perfect) tense. It has two possible endings, _-լ_/_-l_ and _-ր_/_-r_. In Middle Armenian, it can also be used adjectivally.

Note that the resultative, past and perfect participles can be used to form the secondary compound tenses (with the auxiliary _լինել/linel_ “to be” in any tense or mood). The resultative participle can form secondary compound tenses also with other verbs, such as _կենալ_/_kenal_ “to stay”, _կայ_/_kay_ “to exist, to be” or _ունիմ_/_ownim_ “have”. 

Participles will have [Aspect](), [Voice]() and [Subcat]().

#### Examples

* _քաղած_/_k’aġaç_ “plucked”, _բերած_/_beraç_ “brought”, _պարգևված_/_pargewvaç_ “granted, gifted”, _խառնուած_/_xaṙnowaç_ “mixed”, _փռված_/_p’ṙvaç_ “spread” (resultative participle, in different voices),
* _ստեղծող_/_steġçoġ_ “(smth.) creating”, _նախշող_/_naxšoġ_ “decorating”, _խնդացող_/_xndac’oġ_ “laughing”, _թուլացնող_/_t’owlac’noġ_ “weakening”, _կարմրցնող_/_karmrc’noġ_ “reddening” (subject participle, in different voices),
* _տեսնում_/_tesnowm_ “see(ing)”, _խօսում_/_xòsowm_ “speak(ing)”, _հեռանալիս_/_heṙanalis_ “leav(ing)”, _դողալիս_/_doġalis_ “trembl(ing)” (imperfective participle, in different voices),
* _տանելու_/_tanelow_ “for taking”, _չորացնելու_/_čorac’nelow_ “for drying”, _անցանելու_/_anc’anelow_ “for passing”, _գնալու_/_gnalow_ “for going”, _ճանաչելի_/_č̣anačeli_ “smth. that can be recognized” (future participle, in different voices),
* _լցեալ_/_lc’eal_ “filled”, _հեծեալ_/_heçeal_ “mounted”, _մեռեալ_/_meṙeal_ “dead”, _բացուեալ_/_bac’oweal_ “opened” (past participle, in different voices)
* _ասել_/_asel_ “have told”, _մնացել_/_mnac’el_ “have remained”, _հագեր_/_hager_ “have dressed”, _արբեցուցեր_/_arbec’owc’er_ “have made (someone) drunk” (perfect participle, in different voices).
