### Definition
In Middle Persian, auxiliaries are function words that accompany the lexical verb of a verb phrase and expresses grammatical distinctions not carried by the lexical verb, viz. person, number, tense, and mood. All auxiliary verbs have a full verb variant.<br>Auxiliary verbs include the copula, but modal verbs are annotated as full [verbs](VERB) because they have their own case frame (oblique subjects) and display peculiar syntax.

As to their morphological formation, auxiliary verbs do not differ from full verbs, yet we follow UD in annotating a flat structure of the auxiliary complex (see [cop]() and [aux]()).

We find written evidence for some auxiliaries becoming enclitics.

### Examples

- copula verb *būdan* following the resultative participle for marking person and number of the so-called 'past' tense: *raft hēnd* 'they went'
- *ēstādan* "stand" following the resultative participle for the formation of present and past perfect: *raft ēstēnd* 'they have gone'

### Border cases
- The copula verb *būdan* is also used as the existential verb 'to be there, to exist' and it is homophonous with 'to become' except for the so-called 'present tense' (*h-* 'be' vs. *baw-* 'become').
- *ēstādan* can also be a full verb with the preceding participle as a predicative (e.g. 'to stand prepapred' i.e. 'to stand ready').
- *būdan* 'become' is always annotated as a full verb. In some constructions, a passive interpretation emerges but *būdan* has not been ascertained as a passive auxiliary yet.
