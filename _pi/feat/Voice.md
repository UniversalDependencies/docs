Voice is a feature of verbs in Pāli.

### <a name="Act">`Act`</a>: active voice

In Pāli, the active voice is traditionally called *parassapada* ("a word for another"). The subject of the verb is the doer of the action (agent), and the object (in accusative) is typically affected by the action (patient).

#### Examples

- _Dāso odanaṃ <b>pacati</b>_ “The servant <b>cooks</b> the rice.”
- _Tassa etad <b>ahosi</b>_ "This <b>occurred</b> to him."
- _Na vatthaṁ ratanaṁ <b>makkheti</b>_ "The cloth doesn't <b>smear</b> the jewel."

### <a name="Mid">`Mid`</a>: middle voice

In Pāli, the middle voice is traditionally called *attanopada* ("a word for one's self"). It signifies an action performed by the subject reflexively: that is, for their own benefit.

#### Examples

- _<b>Jāyate</b>_ “He is born”
- _So <b>jiyyetha</b>_ "He may, himself, grow old."
- _Pecca so <b>labhate</b> sukhaṁ_ "After death, he <b>obtains for himself</b> happiness."

### <a name="Pass">`Pass`</a>: passive voice

The passive voice indicates that the subject of the verb is affected by the action (patient). In Pāli, the passive base is generally formed by adding the suffix *-ya-* or *-īya-* to the verbal root, and the agent of the action (if present) is typically put in the Instrumental case.

#### Examples

- _Cittaṃ <b>vimucci</b>_ "The mind <b>was freed</b>."
- _Odano <b>paccati</b>_ “The rice <b>is cooking</b>.” (Passive *-cya-* = *-cca-* lets us know that the rice is being cooked, not doing the cooking!)

### <a name="Cau">`Cau`</a>: causative voice

The causative voice is used to mean that the subject of the sentence _caused_ someone else to do the action. In Pāli, causatives are formed by adding the causative infixes (*-e-*, *-aya-*, *-āpe-*, or *-āpaya-*) after the verbal root.

#### Examples

- _Sapathañca maṁ <b>kāresi</b>_ "She <b>made</b> me <b>swear</b> an oath."
- _Te Vipassī Bhagavā dhammiyā kathāya <b>sandassesi</b>_ "The Blessed One Vipassī <b>caused</b> them <b>to see accordingly</b> by speaking about the Dhamma."

### <a name="CauCau">`CauCau`</a>: double causative voice

Pāli exhibits a distinct double causative voice where some causative verbs have come to mean actions that you can have someone else do.

It is constructed by attaching a second causative infix (typically *-āpe-*) to a verb base that is already in a causative form (typically with *-e-*). It denotes an action where a first party causes a second party to get a third party to do something.

#### Examples

- _<b>ropāpeti</b>_ “has [someone] plant [a plant]”
  (From _√ruh_ “to grow” → causative _ropeti_ “causes to grow / plants” → double causative _ropāpeti_ = “gets someone to plant”).
- _<b>kārāpeti</b>_ “causes to get (something) done”
  (From _karoti_ “to do” → causative _kāreti_ “has [something] done [by another]” → double causative _kārāpeti_ “causes [someone] to have [something] done”).
- _Rājaputto ca purohitaputto ca yānāni <b>yojāpetvā</b> yānehi niyyiṁsu_ "The prince and the high priest's son, <b>having had</b> the carriages <b>yolked</b>, set out by carriage." (*yuñjati* "joins" → *yojeti* "causes to join = harnesses" → *yojāpeti* "causes [others] to harness")
