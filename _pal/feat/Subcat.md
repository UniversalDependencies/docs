This feature takes the following values: Intr, Tran.

Ambitransitive verbs are annotated as either transitive or intransitive based on the morpho-syntactic and semantic evidence.

### <a name='Intr'>`Intr`: Intransitive
Intransitive verbs always show the same case frame with verb forms derived from the so-called present and past stem.

- Intransitive verbs and middle verbs: subject in direct case and in agreement with the verb.<br>_ā=š **rōz** čiyōn ōšmārīhēd_ "How, then, are **the days** counted by him?"
- Modal verbs and experiencer verbs: subject in oblique case, second participant in direct case and in agreement with the verb (typical for modal verbs and experiencer verbs).<br>_**u=mān** abāyēd rawāgēnīd ān čē=š kām_ "and we must fulfil that what (is) his wish" (lit. fulfilling that is necessary for us)


### <a name='Tran'>`Tran`: Transitive
Transitive verbs show accusative alignment with verb forms derived from the so-called present stem, and ergative alignment with verb forms derives from the so-called past stem. The ergative construction can be interpreted as either active or passive.
- subject (_man_) in agreement with the verb (_-ēm_): _ud nūn **man** az tō se frašn pursēm_ "and now I am going to ask you three riddles"
- object (_ašmāh_) in agreement with the verb (_hēd_): _wizīd=uš **hēd ašmā** u=š druyist kird **hēd**_ "he (obl.) has chosen **you** and he (obl.) has made **(you)** well"
- passive interpretation: _ōy kē **xwadāymard** az=iš āzard ēstēd dūr waštan_ "The one by whom **the master** has been hurt is to be removed!"
